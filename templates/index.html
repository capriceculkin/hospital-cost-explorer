Who
pays?

The care hospitals give and never get paid for, and who ends up holding the bill.

Every hospital that takes Medicare files a cost report: what it spent, what it charged, what it collected, and the care it gave that nobody paid for. 6,064 hospitals filed one for 2022. Each question in the list opens into its own story.

Pick a state at the top of the list and every story redraws for that state. Almost everything moves when you touch it.

Where the numbers come from

CMS Hospital Provider Cost Report, fiscal year 2022, public domain. Unpaid care is the cost of uncompensated care (charity care plus bad debt). The data is also available as JSON at /api/hospitals. Made by Caprice Culkin, MS Health Informatics, Yale School of Public Health.

How this is counted

Each line is the average hospital of that ownership type. Per bed divides each hospital's unpaid care by its beds; % of revenue divides it by patient revenue. Hospitals that left unpaid care blank count as zero, the same as the cost report total.

    Every hospital is a ball on the court.

    Further right, more of its revenue goes to unpaid care. Below the white line, it lost money in 2022. Drag the sideline to decide how much unpaid care is too much. Anything past it and below break-even is out. Tap a ball to pin it.

    How the court is drawn

    Left to right is unpaid care as a share of patient revenue, on a square-root scale so the long tail fits. Up and down is net margin, kept between -60% and +40%; a few hospitals with bigger losses sit on the bottom edge. Only hospitals that report unpaid care, revenue and net income are on the court.
